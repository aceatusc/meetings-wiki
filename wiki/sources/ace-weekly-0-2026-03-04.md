## One-Line Summary

The meeting sharpened the LLM creativity study around a productivity-creativity tradeoff, creative agency, and a cleaner presentation structure for a qualifying exam or paper talk.

## Key Takeaways

- The opening should be much shorter. The audience already understands that current human-AI collaboration has problems.
- The presentation should focus on the human-side study first: how humans respond when LLMs assist creative programming work.
- The broad bidirectional-collaboration framing can stay, but the current work studies one direction. Do not over-explain the entire thesis agenda in the talk.
- RQ1 should lead to the [[productivity-creativity-tradeoff|productivity-creativity tradeoff]]: LLM-assisted solutions appear more correct and complete under limited time, but not clearly more diverse or creatively distinctive.
- Code metrics like lines of code and function calls should be framed carefully. They show differences between conditions, but they do not automatically measure usefulness or creativity.
- Maintainability may be the cleanest bridge to usefulness because unmaintainable code is hard for another programmer to use.
- RQ2 should explain why the tradeoff may happen: participants spent less time in ideation and experienced fewer creative or aha moments in LLM-assisted sessions.
- Collaboration modes should be labeled as AI roles, not human roles. Candidate modes include brainstormer, verifier, implementer, and a fuller AI-driver/backseat pattern.
- The term cognitive alignment or cognitive misalignment needs a definition. Alternatives suggested in the discussion include control, orchestration, and mixed initiative.
- Slide design should match the spoken story: if a point is on the slide, discuss it; if it will not be discussed, remove it.

## Study Details Captured

- Participants: 20 final participants, recruited from 23 after 3 dropped.
- Design: between-subjects experiment with programming tasks in assisted and unassisted conditions.
- Tasks: algorithmic and system design programming tasks.
- Assisted condition: GitHub Copilot available, external resources restricted.
- Data: screen recordings, think-aloud, final generated solutions, retrospective interviews.
- Analyses: action/behavioral coding, thematic analysis, code characteristics, correctness, completeness, solution uniqueness, code similarity, and creative moment coding.
