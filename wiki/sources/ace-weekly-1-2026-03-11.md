## One-Line Summary

The meeting discussed a lightweight co-design path for the spreadsheet-agent prototype, evaluation measures, IRB constraints, and near-term workshop writing tasks.

## Key Takeaways

- A co-design or participatory-design round can be lightweight. Experts can review the current tool, give feedback, and discuss redesign ideas in a short call.
- Suggested scale was roughly 6 to 10 people, with 8 to 10 treated as sufficient for rich co-design data.
- The process does not need to be described as an overly rigorous empirical method if the goal is to avoid design fixation and gather expert ideas.
- Experts can work over a prototype, video, screenshot, XML file, or sketchable artifact, then share comments or sketches.
- Stay within the already approved IRB/formative-study scope when changing the study procedure.
- Evaluation should avoid becoming a benchmark of agent quality. The focus should be how well users understand, verify, and detect issues.
- Candidate measures include time, accuracy of detecting injected issues, understanding of changes, and satisfaction.
- For workshop/proposal planning, the group discussed co-working, a paragraph, activities, collaborators, and submission timing.

## Decisions and Direction

- Co-design can be framed as expert review plus design discussion rather than a heavy standalone method.
- The evaluation should distinguish user issue-detection accuracy from agent task accuracy.
- The work should stay model-agnostic because underlying agents will keep improving.

## Action Items

- Prepare materials for expert review or co-design.
- Clarify evaluation measures and whether issues will be injected into tasks.
- Make sure any co-design procedure fits IRB/formative-study language.
- Work on the workshop/proposal paragraph, activities, and collaborator plan.
